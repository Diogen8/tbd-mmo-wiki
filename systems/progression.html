Progression

Three separate axes, deliberately not stacked on the same power source, so they never compete or duplicate each other.

On this page
  1. Core idea
  2. The passive tree
  3. Reset (soft prestige)
  4. Why affinities never gate
  5. Crafting stones
  6. Anti-grind

A team brainstorm, ongoing since September 2026. Not agreed for build yet.

Core idea

  1. Passive tree: non-combat and low-power; it shapes how fast you grow. Inspired by Path of Exile’s tree, but the tree itself carries almost no direct power.
  2. Use-based weapon mastery: fight with a one-handed sword, get better with one-handed swords. Diminishing returns per kill and target resist grinding. Gains come from hits the server confirms, not from what a client reports. Mastery thresholds unlock weapon-specific active skills.
  3. Crafting, through Novice and Master stones: governs crafting skill growth specifically; a concrete two-item, three-tier system described below.

The rough split: stones set the floor and raise the ceiling, practice fills in the ceiling, and the passive tree sets the direction, meaning how fast you grow and toward what.

The passive tree

  • Nodes are diplomacy, trade and survival, explicitly not combat. Combat power comes from weapon mastery, not from the tree. That is what makes a full tree wipe on reset tolerable: resetting costs social and economic reach, never your ability to fight.
  • Individual nodes give almost nothing numerically. Weight comes from breadth and from qualitative unlocks (dialogue options, trade routes, recipes), not from stacking small percentages. A numerically weak node still needs to do something distinct, or the tree feels pointless even in aggregate. The node catalogue is not designed yet.
  • Affinity nodes don’t grant power directly. They raise an affinity (for example Light) that speeds up mastery gain for related skills, and possibly crafting-stone effectiveness too (an open question). This ties the tree to the other systems instead of leaving it standalone.
    • Affinities must accelerate, never gate. Zero affinity in something still trains it, just slower. This is the one hard constraint; see below.
  • Level-up gives a small stat increment and one tree point. Linear, and not the main power source.

Reset (soft prestige)

  • At level 100, resetting is optional. A player can stay at 100 indefinitely and keep growing through gear and weapon mastery. It is not a gate on further progression.
  • On reset, level returns to 1 and the tree wipes completely and must be replanned. Weapon mastery and crafting-stone progress are not touched: nothing about combat or crafting capability is lost.
  • What is kept: a flat, one-time permanent bonus to the tree’s point budget per reset, currently framed as +1. Growth across resets is additive, not multiplicative, so it doesn’t blow out into an unbounded veteran-versus-newcomer gap.
  • The narrative payoff: a character can genuinely redirect across a “second life”. Level to 100 as a warrior, then respec the tree toward Light affinity on the second pass, without losing what the first life built.

Why affinities must never gate

EVE Online once had almost this exact mechanic: eleven “Learning” skills that boosted training speed for everything else. CCP removed them in December 2010. They had become a mandatory tax new players felt they had to train before doing anything else, they added no real strategic choice, and they let dedicated players skip ahead of the intended pace (Learning skills are going away).

An affinity of zero must mean “slower”, never “blocked” or so slow that it is effectively blocked.

Crafting: Novice and Master stones

Two consumable items, both made of crystallized mana. See Cosmology for what that means in the world, and Mana Economy for where their material comes from.

Sets the floor

Novice Stone

Equip it and it passively teaches a crafting skill up to a soft threshold of acceptable competence. EVE’s “learn while you sleep” idea, but capped.

Raises the ceiling

Master Stone

For top-tier mastery. Worn continuously, it slowly raises the practice ceiling rather than granting progress directly. Hands then fill the new headroom.

  • Only hands from there. Past the Novice Stone’s cap, progress needs actual crafting, with the same diminishing-returns shape as weapon mastery. Practice alone has its own natural ceiling and never reaches true mastery, by design.
  • Stone and practice are both required at the top. Neither alone gets there.

Depletion and the single-slot rule

  • Both stones drain just from being equipped. They are crystallized mana, so they constantly leak, whether the wearer is asleep, crafting, fighting or idle. The drain is tied to time worn, not to use.
  • Only one stone, of either tier, can be active at a time, across all skills. This is the specialization lever: pushing a Master Stone toward mastery in one craft means you can’t bootstrap a second skill with a Novice Stone without unequipping the Master Stone, which pauses its ceiling-raising.
  • A tension worth keeping: carrying a rare, draining Master Stone into risky content is a real decision between exposure and progress. For raw mana cores the death rules are settled in PvP, Robbery & PK; whether an equipped stone can be lost the same way is still open.

Anti-grind (weapon mastery)

Diminishing returns per kill aren’t enough on their own; the Elder Scrolls “safe grinding” complaints and Ultima Online macro-training are the failure modes to avoid. At minimum it also needs experience against far-below-level targets tapering toward zero, and/or a per-session or daily soft cap. The exact curve is not designed yet.

Open questions · Progression
  • The reset bonus size (+1 or larger) depends on an earlier decision: should the tree ever be fully completable by a dedicated player, or always stay a trade-off, as in Path of Exile? Pick the philosophy first, then size the number from the total node count.
  • Total tree size and the node catalogue.
  • The exact anti-grind curve for weapon mastery.
  • Do affinity boosts apply only to future training, or can they be reallocated some other way? The current framing is future only.
  • The soft cap for the Novice threshold, and how much headroom a Master Stone opens over what interval.
  • Where do stones come from?
  • Resolved: crafted from monster mana cores; the source core’s quality sets the stone’s tier. Still open: the exact acquisition and crafting cost, and how it interacts with the wider economy.
  • Can a stone actually be lost (death, theft), or is it safe once acquired? That decides how much weight the risk of carrying it really has.
